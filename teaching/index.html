A moment from my topology teaching

A painting that needs
both nails.

I once used a painting hung by a string around two nails to explain the fundamental group of the twice-punctured plane. Remove either nail and the painting falls.

π1(R2 ∖ {P1, P2}) ≅ F(a, b)

The string represents the nontrivial commutator aba−1b−1. Filling either puncture kills its generator and makes this word trivial.

Try the algebra behind the trick

ab

aba⁻¹b⁻¹ ≠ 1

Both punctures are present: the commutator is nontrivial.

A moment from my topology teaching

Two angles.
Then a doughnut break.

When I taught the product topology, I brought doughnuts for the students during the break. A circle has one angular coordinate; a torus has two.

T2 = S1 × S1

Products U × V of open subsets of the two circles form a basis for the product topology. The surface of a doughnut models a torus.

The parametrization
((2 + cos φ) cos θ, (2 + cos φ) sin θ, sin φ)

Changing either angle moves along one circle factor.
